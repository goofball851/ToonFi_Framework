A framework layer for building character and world-based experiences where  
**stories end, but existence continues**.

This repository defines a **logic standard**, not a style guide.

---

## What is the Narrative Layer Stack?

The Narrative Layer Stack governs what happens **before and after a story is finished**.

It applies to:
- web experiences
- physical products
- collectibles
- NFTs
- AI systems
- interactive interfaces

It does **not**:
- generate plot
- branch canon
- optimize engagement
- reward control

---

## Core Promise

> **Stories end, but Existence continues.**

---

## 📖 1. What the Narrative Layer Is

The Narrative Layer is the system that allows a ToonFi world to:

- remember events  
- accumulate history  
- surface lore  
- host canon  
- record personal experiences  
- contextualize episodes  

It is not a script.

It is the **memory structure of the world.**
It is **what the world knows.**

---

## 2. Core Narrative Principle

ToonFi does not treat story as a single timeline.

It treats story as a **layered archive.**

Narrative exists simultaneously as:

- world history  
- object memory  
- character perspective  
- discovered documents  
- recorded episodes  
- personal logs  
- lost or hidden fragments  

The world is not “telling” a story.

It is **containing** one.

---

## 3. Narrative Surfaces

Narrative must appear through **surfaces**, not only text.

Common ToonFi narrative surfaces:

### 🎞️ Episodes  
Authored windows into the world.  
They canonize events and reveal perspectives.

### 📜 Logs & Records  
Notes, transmissions, reports, diaries, lab entries, signals.

### 🧠 Memories  
Dreams, echoes, corrupted data, forgotten scenes, flash spaces.

### 🧱 Environments  
Scars, ruins, changed spaces, locked doors, rebuilt areas.

### 🧬 Objects  
Mutations, wear, inscriptions, states, attachments.

### 👁️ Characters  
Dialogue shifts, behavior changes, recognition, roles.

Narrative should be **discoverable, not delivered.**

---

## 4. Canon Structure

ToonFi supports multiple levels of canon.

### A. World Canon  
What is always true about the world.

- its rules  
- its origin  
- its major events  
- its entities  

This changes slowly.

---

### B. Event Canon  
What has happened.

- resolved phenomena  
- completed arcs  
- collapsed systems  
- archived broadcasts  

This accumulates.

---

### C. Episodic Canon  
What episodes have shown.

- character perspectives  
- story fragments  
- localized conflicts  
- thematic arcs  

Episodes **add**, they don’t overwrite.

---

### D. Personal Canon  
What a visitor has encountered.

- discovered locations  
- unlocked objects  
- witnessed phases  
- chosen alignments  

This is stored per identity.

The world may treat different visitors differently.

## 5. Narrative Objects

Some ToonFi objects are explicitly narrative.

Examples:

- memory shards  
- corrupted logs  
- story devices  
- recorders  
- witnesses  
- archivists  
- historians  
- living books  

They exist to:

- store events  
- reveal context  
- change interpretation  
- unlock deeper layers  

Narrative objects allow story to be **played with** without branching plots.

---

## 6. Time & Continuity

ToonFi narrative supports:

- non-linear discovery  
- out-of-order episodes  
- layered timelines  
- recurring events  
- forgotten phases  
- retold histories  

Continuity is not “watch order.”

Continuity is:

> what the world can still remember.

Some things may be:

- permanent  
- seasonal  
- cyclical  
- corrupted  
- lost  
- rumored  

The Narrative Layer defines those rules.

---

## 7. Relationship to Phenomena

Phenomena generate narrative.

They:

- create events  
- alter objects  
- unlock logs  
- spawn episodes  
- change spaces  

The Narrative Layer:

- records them  
- contextualizes them  
- archives them  
- re-surfaces them  
- lets them be interpreted  

Phenomena are the motion.  
Narrative is the trace.

---

## 8. Relationship to Episodes

Episodes are not “the story.”

They can:

- depict events  
- dramatize perspectives  
- misrepresent history  
- foreshadow phenomena  
- canonize changes  
- unlock archives  

After an episode:

- something should be newly understood  
- newly visible  
- newly archived  
- or newly questionable  

The Narrative Layer absorbs the episode into the world.

---

## 9. Narrative State Types

A ToonFi world’s narrative elements can be:

- **Known** – visible to all  
- **Hidden** – exists but undiscovered  
- **Personal** – tied to identity  
- **World** – shared continuity  
- **Unstable** – may change  
- **Archived** – locked but readable  
- **Lost** – referenced but inaccessible  

These states allow mystery without confusion.

---

## 10. Narrative Progression

Narrative growth happens when:

- phenomena resolve  
- episodes release  
- areas unlock  
- objects mutate  
- logs appear  
- characters change  

Not when a “chapter” is completed.

Story is a **byproduct of world evolution.**

---

## 11. Design Rules

The Narrative Layer should:

- reward curiosity  
- support re-visiting  
- tolerate out-of-order discovery  
- preserve mystery  
- allow contradiction  
- store residue  
- never require full completion  

No user should ever feel:

“I missed the story.”

They should feel:

“There is more.”

---

## 12. Narrative Success Check

The Narrative Layer is working if:

- the world feels older than the visitor  
- events leave traces  
- objects imply stories  
- characters acknowledge history  
- episodes feel like windows, not containers  
- visitors build personal continuity  
- mystery is preserved  

---

## 13. One-Line Definition

The ToonFi Narrative Layer is:

> **the memory system that allows cartoon worlds to accumulate history, perspective, and meaning over time.**
